**Audited:** 2026-08-07
**Branch:** `codex/reader-evidence-grammar`
**Scope:** Fresh Greens, Navi, UnderstandingFAFSA, and TikTok inside Reader Mode
**Method:** Internal 90-second retrieval simulation followed by a 10-minute argument review against the typed chapter evidence maps

## Status and limitation

This is an internal evaluator pass, not a substitute for observing independent hiring managers. It tests whether the current page order, headings, orientation block, dominant claims, dominant proofs, outcomes, and caveats make the intended answers available without relying on private project context.

The outside-reader protocol at the end of this document is the remaining human-validation step.

## 90-second retrieval simulation

A project passes the internal scan when the first screen and chapter trail make six facts recoverable:

1. Myles's role.
2. The affected person or operating problem.
3. The strongest design decision.
4. The proof a reviewer should remember.
5. What exists or shipped.
6. What the work does not yet prove.

## Cross-portfolio differentiation

The four stories now make different hiring arguments:

- **Fresh Greens:** research-to-product reasoning plus solo design and engineering.
- **Navi:** research synthesis, system thinking, and a working browser product.
- **UnderstandingFAFSA:** operational constraints, modular content systems, and qualified outcome reporting.

This differentiation is the main defense against the shared Reader system becoming a reskinned template.

## Outside-reader protocol

Use at least three readers who have not seen the portfolio work. Product designers, design managers, recruiters, and adjacent hiring partners are all useful, but record each reader's role.

### 90-second scan

Give the reader one project with no explanation. Stop after 90 seconds and ask:

1. What was Myles's role?
2. Who or what problem was the work for?
3. What was the most important decision?
4. What evidence do you remember?
5. What exists or shipped?
6. What remains uncertain or unproven?

Do not correct the reader during the exercise. Record omissions and incorrect inferences verbatim.

A project passes when at least two of three readers accurately recover role, problem, decision, proof, and outcome, and none make a material causal or ownership inference that the page should have prevented.

### 10-minute read

After a full read, ask:

1. Where did the reasoning become unclear?
2. Which artifact was most convincing?
3. Which artifact felt unnecessary?
4. Where did you infer more than the evidence established?
5. What would you ask Myles in an interview?
6. What is the one sentence you remember about the project?

Classify each miss as orientation, ownership, claim, proof, interpretation, caveat, pacing, media legibility, interaction, or performance.

### Change-control rule

Only revise a frozen project when the review exposes a specific comprehension, evidence, accessibility, media-legibility, interaction, or performance failure. Do not reopen a project for generalized aesthetic churn.
